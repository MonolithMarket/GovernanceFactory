## NM-001 (MEDIUM) — tranche 0 emissions burn from the block of deployment

Re-traced and now quantified (PoC `testTrancheZeroEmissionsBurnFromTheBlockOfDeployment`).
`_deployCoinDAO` calls `fundNextTranche()` at `CoinDAOFactory.sol:302`, starting a 365-day GOV
stream while `StakingRewards._totalSupply == 0`. On the `deploy()` path the Coin market is created
in the *same transaction*, so `coin.totalSupply()` is provably 0 and no staker can exist yet:

| | Value |
| :-- | --: |
| Tranche 0 | 2,123,121 GOV |
| Burned by a 7-day gap before the first staker | **40,717.39 GOV** (1.9% of the tranche) |
| Reachable afterwards | 2,082,403.61 GOV |

The loss runs at ~5,817 GOV per idle day and is **unrecoverable**: `recoverERC20` was intentionally
omitted and `renounceOwnership()` runs at `CoinDAOFactory.sol:303`, so nothing can reclaim it.

`rewardPerToken()` returns `rewardPerTokenStored` unchanged while supply is zero, and the first
staker's `updateReward` pre-hook jumps `lastUpdateTime` forward to the current timestamp. So the
elapsed emission is not merely unearned — it is discarded rather than carried forward, and no later
staker can reach it.

**Why this one is not covered by the existing comment.** `StakingRewards.sol:138-140` documents the
behaviour as accepted, on the stated grounds that *"the window between tranche funding and the first
staker is expected to be short."* That holds for `deployForExistingCoin`, where the Coin already has
holders. It cannot hold for `deploy()`, where the market is minted in the same transaction and the
window is bounded below by however long it takes the first borrower to mint Coin and stake it.

**Suggested fixes**, in rough order of preference:

1. Gate the funder on live supply — one line, no schedule change:
   ```solidity
   // StakingRewardsFunder.fundNextTranche()
   if (stakingRewards.totalSupply() == 0) revert NoStakersYet();
   ```
   The tranche schedule is already permissionless and self-pacing, so the first tranche simply
   starts when the market is live.
2. Drop `fundNextTranche()` from `_deployCoinDAO` and let anyone call it once the market has
   stakers. This also removes a CREATE-heavy step from an already large deployment transaction.
3. If funding at deployment is required, accept the loss explicitly and correct the comment at
   `StakingRewards.sol:138-140` to say the window is unbounded on the `deploy()` path.

### NM-012 — quorum equals the proposal threshold

```solidity
// CoinDAOFactory.sol:36-37
uint256 public constant GOVERNOR_PROPOSAL_THRESHOLD = GOV_TOKEN_SUPPLY / 1_000;
uint256 public constant GOVERNOR_QUORUM             = GOV_TOKEN_SUPPLY / 1000;   // same value
```

Raised as HIGH on the grounds that a holder of 0.1% becomes a self-sufficient majority: with
`GovernorCountingSimple`, `_quorumReached` counts For+Abstain and `_voteSucceeded` needs only
`For > Against`, so a proposer at threshold satisfies both alone.

**Accepted as intended.** This is majority rule operating as specified, not a defect. The
supporting PoC makes the point better than the finding did:
`testHonestVoterMustMatchTheAttackerOneForOneToBlockIt` shows the defender loses because they hold
*less* stake — which is what a vote is. Nothing here is specific to this codebase: an unopposed
holder of a quorum-sized position wins in any Governor deployment configured this way, and the
low quorum is the deliberate remedy for the NM-007 launch blackout.

Characterization tests retained (`testQuorumEqualsProposalThreshold`,
`testLoneProposerAtThresholdCapturesTheTreasuryUnopposed`,
`testLoneProposerAtThresholdSeizesTheLenderManagerRole`,
`testAttackerUnstakesAfterVotingAndProposalStillSucceeds`) — they document the reachable governance
surface at these parameters, including that a lone threshold-sized proposal reaches both the
timelock's liquid GOV (326,732) and the Monolith lender `manager` role, and that sGOV's lack of a
lock-up means the position need only be held from `propose` until the snapshot.

**One non-security note.** The two constants are written `1_000` and `1000` — the same value with
different digit grouping, three lines apart. That is a legibility hazard rather than a
vulnerability: a future edit to either is easy to make without noticing the relationship. If the
equality is deliberate, deriving one from the other states it in code:
```solidity
uint256 public constant GOVERNOR_QUORUM = GOVERNOR_PROPOSAL_THRESHOLD;
```

### NM-015 — revenue is deferred one period before it streams

Since `3847f71`, revenue notified during period *N* is queued and streams during period *N+1*, so
it is paid to whoever is staked then rather than to the stakers present while it accrued.

**Accepted as intended, and my analysis of it was wrong.** I reported it as a systematic transfer
from long-term stakers to newcomers, measured at 87% in a PoC. That framing does not survive the
control test.

`testDeferralIsNeutralOverAFullTenureOnceRevenueIsFlowing` measures a staker joining a *running*
system at a boundary and holding two full periods:

| | Value |
| :-- | --: |
| Revenue accrued during his tenure | 98,000.00 |
| His stake-weighted fair share | 49,000.00 |
| **Actually earned** | **49,000.00** (to rounding dust) |

A staker who joins at a boundary **inherits** the previous period's accrual, and that windfall
exactly offsets the period forfeited on exit. Over a full tenure the deferral is neutral. The 87%
figure in my original write-up came from the *launch* case, where the first staker has no
predecessor period to inherit from — it was a launch artifact, not a steady-state property, and
I presented it as the latter.

The author's rationale also holds on its own terms: the fixed one-period exit cost is a real
lock-in incentive, and it shrinks as a fraction of total earnings the longer a staker remains —
100% of accrual if you stake one period, ~2% if you stake fifty-two. That is coherent mechanism
design, not an accident of the NM-011 fix.

**Residual, INFO only.** The launch cohort bears the one-period cost with nothing inherited to
offset it: whoever is staked during the first period forfeits that period's accrual to the second
cohort. Bounded at one `rewardsDuration` (7 days) of revenue, one time, self-correcting thereafter.
`testExitingAtAPeriodBoundaryForfeitsAFullPeriodOfRevenue` is retained as the characterization of
this launch case, now correctly labelled as such.

If the deferral is intentional, it is worth a comment in `StakedGovToken` in the same style as
`StakingRewards.sol:138-140` — its absence is what led two passes of this audit to treat it as a
defect.

### NM-006 — the lender operator role is permanently held by `RevenueRouter`

Raised as MEDIUM in both prior runs; confirmed intended. The router holds the lender `operator`
role and exposes `acceptLenderOperator()` and `setManager()` but no `setPendingOperator`, so the
role cannot be migrated by anyone, including the timelock. Together with the immutable `treasury`
and `govStaking`, the revenue path is fixed at launch and `govStakingBps` is the only revenue
parameter governance can change.

`testLenderOperatorRoleIsIntentionallyPermanent` is retained as a regression test pinning that
intent — it fails if a future change adds a migration path.

## Verified sound (actively challenged this pass, no finding)

- **NM-010 and NM-011 are genuinely fixed.** Re-ran `test/NemesisPoC2.t.sol` against the new source:
  the Monolith allocation is bound to the immutable `monolithTreasury` on both deployment paths, and
  1-wei dust notifications no longer move `periodFinish` or dilute `rewardRate`.
- **NM-014 — CREATE-nonce prediction across RLP length boundaries: FALSE POSITIVE.** Hypothesis was
  that `_predictCreateAddress`'s RLP encoding might break once the factory nonce crosses the
  single-byte boundaries at 0x7f/0xff, permanently bricking the factory after ~13 launches. PoC
  `testCreateNoncePredictionHoldsAcrossRlpLengthBoundaries` runs 30 consecutive launches to a
  factory nonce of **271**, crossing both boundaries; every predicted `RevenueRouter` address
  matches. `_nextCreateNonce` also stays in lockstep with the real EVM nonce — all ten CREATEs per
  launch are paired with a `_recordCreate()`, and library calls are DELEGATECALL so the CREATEs are
  attributed to the factory.
- **Reward solvency holds under the new queueing design.** `queuedRewards` only grows from amounts
  transferred in first (`notifyRewardAmount`), from the un-streamed tail of a fully funded period
  (`withdrawTo`), or from `_startReward`'s own re-queue paths. Balance ≥ queued + accrued at every
  point; `_startReward`'s balance guard cannot spuriously re-queue.
- **`rewardPerToken()` cannot underflow.** Re-proved `lastTimeRewardApplicable() >= lastUpdateTime`
  across all four mutation sites under the restructured control flow, including the new
  `_startQueuedRewardsIfReady` post-hook.
- **An active reward period with zero staked supply is still unreachable.** Periods start only behind
  `totalSupply() != 0` (`StakedGovToken.sol:216`), and `withdrawTo` closes the period when supply
  hits zero (`:117-127`). `withdrawTo` is the only burn path.
- **The `updateReward` post-hook does not corrupt per-user checkpoints.** `_startReward` writes
  `lastUpdateTime` and `periodFinish` but never `rewardPerTokenStored`, so a `userRewardPerTokenPaid`
  written in the pre-hook stays valid.
- **`notifyRewardAmount` lacking `nonReentrant`** (every other mutating entrypoint has it) is not
  exploitable: the underlying is our own plain `GovToken`, and the only reachable reentry would be
  through a hook-bearing Coin, which leaves total queued and total transferred equal.
- **Quorum checkpointing** — `_optimisticUpperLookupRecent` discards `latestCheckpoint()`'s `exists`
  flag, which would return quorum 0 on an empty trace, but the constructor's `_setQuorum` always
  pushes and rejects 0, so the trace is never empty. Historical `quorum(timepoint)` queries remain
  stable.
- **Allocation math is exact** — the five allocations sum to `GOV_TOKEN_SUPPLY` for every
  `deployerStakeBps` in range; the factory retains nothing.
- **Privilege handoff and `deployForExistingCoin` authorisation** re-traced and hold.

## Note on methodology

Both prior runs recorded a `via-ir` timestamp trap: a local `uint256 t0 = block.timestamp` is
re-materialised from the TIMESTAMP opcode on each use, so `vm.warp(t0 + i * 1 days)` compounds
instead of stepping. `test/NemesisPoC3.t.sol` derives all time arithmetic from the compile-time
constant `T0`. **This has now produced one false negative and one false positive across three
audits and should be written into the repo's test conventions.**

Two analytical errors from this pass are worth recording, because both were caught by controls
rather than by review:

1. The first NM-015 draft claimed a *just-in-time* attack — that `queuedRewards` accumulates
   unboundedly between periods and a whale could deposit later to capture the pot. The PoC
   disproved it: `distribute()` carries `updateReward`, whose post-hook starts any eligible period,
   so the queue self-drains and exposure is capped at one period.
2. The surviving NM-015 claim — a systematic transfer from long-term to short-term stakers — was
   also wrong, and this one reached the report. It rested on a single measurement of the *launch*
   case, where the first staker has no predecessor period to inherit from. The obvious control
   (a staker joining a running system) was not run until the author pushed back, and it shows the
   effect is neutral over a full tenure. **A measurement of a boundary case was generalised to a
   steady-state claim without running the steady-state test.**

The general lesson for the next pass: when a finding's mechanism is "X gets less than Y", run the
symmetric case before writing it up. Both errors here were one test away from being caught.

## Summary

- **1 open finding: NM-001 (MEDIUM)** — ~5,817 GOV per idle day burned unrecoverably, with a
  one-line fix available.
- **NM-010 and NM-011 confirmed fixed** by `3615738` and `3847f71`. NM-007 downgraded MEDIUM → LOW
  by `9ea848c`. No regression from any of the three commits survived review.
- **NM-012 and NM-015 accepted as intended design**; NM-006 withdrawn for the same reason. Their
  tests are retained as characterization and regression coverage rather than as exploits.
- **2 hypotheses falsified**, one of them after publication (NM-015's steady-state claim) and one
  before (NM-014, CREATE-nonce prediction).
- Test suite: **67/67 passing**, including 11 tests in `test/NemesisPoC3.t.sol`.

Standing recommendation from this pass, independent of the findings: the three commits reviewed
here each changed a parameter or an ordering that other parts of the system depended on, and in
each case the dependency was implicit. Whatever is decided about NM-001, the two design intentions
confirmed in this session — quorum's relationship to the proposal threshold, and the one-period
reward deferral — are currently recorded nowhere in `src/`. A comment at each site would have
saved two of this pass's three candidate findings.
